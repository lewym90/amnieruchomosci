Verification Report

A document you can put on the table and show your family.

The report is analytical: it sets out facts established from documents and sources, compares them and analyses them. Every finding states its source and the date and time it was checked. The buyer receives the report in print, in a folder, before signing the preliminary agreement. We deliver the report within 5 working days of your order. If an office or a housing cooperative takes longer to issue a document, we note this in the report and send a supplement.

  • It is not a legal opinion or advice of any kind.
  • It is not a valuation. The value of a property is determined by a licensed valuer.
  • It contains no recommendation. The decision is yours alone.

Kraków and the surrounding area

Here, a listing won’t tell you everything.

The Kraków market has its own pitfalls. Besides the land register and the owners’ association’s documents, we check things that in this region can change a property’s value or a buyer’s plans.

  • 01

    Tenement houses and heritage

    Entry in the register or the municipal list of historic buildings, conservation zones and the rules of the Old Town Cultural Park. They decide which works need the conservator’s consent and what may be changed on the facade.

  • 02

    Flood risk

    We check the flood hazard maps for the Vistula and its tributaries: the Wilga, Prądnik, Dłubnia and Serafa. This matters for safety and for the cost of insurance.

  • 03

    Balice airport

    A restricted-use area applies around Kraków-Balice airport. We check whether the property lies within it and what that means for noise and building.

  • 04

    Old mine workings

    Around Wieliczka and Bochnia we check whether the property is within reach of the old salt mine workings.

  • 05

    Housing cooperatives

    A cooperative ownership right to a flat often has no land register entry. We establish the legal position with the cooperative and check whether it is worth opening a register before the sale.

  • 06

    What may be built next door

    We check whether a local zoning plan covers the area and, if not, which planning decisions have been issued nearby.

Rules and FAQ

Rules, regulations and answers to common questions.

We operate within Polish law on real estate management, personal data protection, consumer rights, and anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing.

How much does it cost?

There is one published rate: [rate]. On a sale, the fee is paid by the seller only, once the sale agreement has been signed as a notarial deed. We accept payment by bank transfer only. For difficult cases, the preparatory stage is priced separately. The Verification Report for buyers has a fixed price of PLN 599 for a flat or PLN 899 for a house, and is delivered within 5 working days.

Is the Verification Report legal advice or a legal opinion?

No. The report is an analytical document: it sets out facts established from documents and the sources listed, compares and analyses them. It is not a legal opinion, advice of any kind or a valuation, and it contains no recommendation. Where legal help is needed, the right person is an advocate, a legal adviser or a notary.

Are you insured?

Yes. A real estate broker in Poland must hold third-party liability insurance for damage caused in connection with brokerage activities. Each of us has our own policy. We give the policy numbers on this website, in the agreement and in every report: Michał Lewandowski – policy no. [number], [insurer]; Agata Ceglińska – policy no. [number], [insurer].

Why do you need an identity document?

A real estate broker is an obliged institution under the Polish Act on Counteracting Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing. Before starting work with a client, we must apply customer due diligence measures, including identifying the client and verifying their identity and, in certain cases, establishing the beneficial owner. If these measures cannot be applied, the law does not allow us to work together.

What form does the brokerage agreement take?

Under Polish law, a brokerage agreement must be made in writing or in electronic form, otherwise it is invalid. It sets out the scope of work, the fee and how the fee is calculated. We never sign the agreement at the first meeting.

More questions (7)
Can I withdraw from the agreement?

Yes, if you are a consumer. We have no fixed office, so our agreements are concluded off-premises or at a distance. In that case you may withdraw within 14 days without giving a reason, under the Polish Consumer Rights Act. You will receive information about this right and a model withdrawal form together with the agreement.

If you would like us to start work before that period ends, we will ask you for an explicit request in writing.

How is a price analysis different from a valuation?

A price analysis compares the transaction prices of similar properties nearby, based on prices in notarial deeds rather than in listings. It is not a valuation. Formal valuations are carried out by licensed property valuers.

Why an exclusive agreement?

Before a property goes on the market, we verify it, arrange a photo shoot and prepare a campaign. Without exclusivity we cannot do this properly. The term of the agreement and how it can be terminated are set out in the agreement, which you receive to read before signing.

Which area do you cover?

Kraków and the counties of Kraków, Wieliczka, Myślenice and Bochnia. The full list of municipalities is in the Where we work section.

Which language do you work in?

Meetings and documents are in Polish. If you are more comfortable in another language, we will arrange an interpreter. At the notary, a sworn translator is required by law when a party does not speak Polish, and we organise this in advance.

Can I sell or let a property while living abroad?

Yes. We can handle a sale or letting remotely: we prepare the documents in advance and keep you informed through the weekly report. The notarial deed of sale can be signed on your behalf by an attorney-in-fact.

How is my personal data processed?

Anti-money laundering

As a real estate broker, we are an obliged institution under the Polish Act of 1 March 2018 on Counteracting Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing. This means obligations that apply to every client, regardless of the value of the transaction.

What this means in practice

  • We identify each client and verify their identity on the basis of an identity document.
  • For legal persons and other entities, we establish who is authorised to represent them and who the beneficial owner is.
  • We assess the business relationship and, where justified, establish the purpose and nature of the transaction and the source of funds.
  • We establish whether the client is a politically exposed person.
  • We monitor the business relationship on an ongoing basis.

What we cannot do

If customer due diligence measures cannot be applied, the law does not allow us to start working together or to carry out the transaction. Nor may we inform clients that information has been passed to the General Inspector of Financial Information.
